# Campaign and generated maps

Campaign maps and chapter ordering live in `src/game/campaign.ts`. Quick Match and Chaos Mode use `src/game/generatedMaps.ts`; both load through `createMatch` and share combat, production, controls, and weapon rules with the campaign. All faction colors use the same combat, production, and charging rules.

## Generated battlefields

Both generated modes use a 35-50-planet budget, allowing richer routes while staying compact. The normal minimum is three neighbors. Quick Match requires at least 60% of planets to have exactly four or five neighbors; only explicit corridor entrance worlds may have two (at most three). Chaos validates each star system separately: at least 70% of its planets must have exactly four or five internal neighbors, with an average of at least four. Ordinary worlds retain at least three internal neighbors after rival capitals disappear. Cross-system attacks add routes to these local connections. At map level at least 70% of Chaos planets have four or more neighbors, with a maximum of nine. Both modes use the existing 600-unit attack range and tight rectangular bounds including full swept orbits. Chapter 1 geography is unchanged.

Quick Match connects four five-world capital districts through a continuous outer loop and four inner crossings. Several different worlds lead out of each district; there is no single frontier gate. The capital itself remains protected behind four neutral worlds, including three equally priced cheap expansion choices (12-18 ships) and a reserve (24-30). All four capitals start with 200 ships, and their faction assignments are shuffled. The stationary battlefield stays connected after every rival capital disappears, after all orbiting planets are removed, and after any one additional fixed crossing is removed.

Crossroads uses one interior orbital arena, four outer routes and an optional five-world flank arsenal. Outer Arsenals adds one or two flank arsenals. Twin Fronts and Linked Arenas use two orbital arenas, wider perimeter crossings and a four-world bypass around one outer crossing; the two families use different system separations, changing interior contacts. Single-star recipes have 37-49 planets; twin-star recipes have 50. Population, aspect ratio, arsenal placement, rotation, garrisons, specialties and opening contacts vary with the seed.

Every Quick Match has one or two stars with independent opening phases. All generated compact systems (maximum orbital radius at most 400) complete a turn in 100 seconds. All generated normal and large systems complete a turn in 300 seconds. Timing depends only on physical size, with no random per-star or per-seed speed differences. Current Quick Match arenas all use the compact 100-second period. Chapter 1 periods remain exactly as authored. Five/six/seven-world orbital circuits keep at least four internal neighbors, and each has at least two certified permanent fixed approaches. Optional orbital contacts open and close as the arena rotates, while the fixed outer routes remain usable. Weapon groups contain three to five connected worlds. Production groups permit only Overdrive, defense only Repulse, strike only Omni, and support only Overdrive/Repulse. Every map includes all three single specialties; extra arsenals add support groups. Groups are disjoint, every armed planet belongs to one group, and capital districts start unarmed. Minimum planet spacing is 270, including full swept orbital clearance. Orbiting worlds cannot attack directly into capitals.

Chaos chooses among four composition recipes, rather than forcing the same size mix every time: four classic systems; four varied normal systems; one large system with two normal systems and a small public system; or two large systems with two small public systems. The classic 12-world system and the earlier four-classic-system battlefield remain options. All maps contain four stars, four shuffled faction capitals and at most 50 planets. Classic/normal systems each contain one capital; large systems contain two sufficiently separated capitals. Every world has one or two distinct weapons. Capitals start with 200 ships, share the same weapon combination and have three cheap neutral choices.

New pentagon webs have eight outer worlds and five inner worlds (13 total). Woven large systems use 10-11 outer worlds and 6-7 inner worlds (16-18 total), producing many cross-ring routes instead of long perimeter chains. Classic systems retain eight outer worlds and four staggered inner worlds (12 total). Scattered normal formations perturb that proven geometry within certified bounds. Small five/six/seven-world polygons and scattered variants offer dense public routes. Every formation must meet the internal density rules independently of neighboring systems.

`generated.systems` records each system's size, pattern and membership. Small systems rotate in 100 seconds; normal and large systems rotate in 300 seconds, regardless of pattern, planet count or seed. Weighted center placement accounts for physical system size. Bent fronts, branching constellations, circuits and wide fronts vary the overall geography. Public systems always connect to at least two existing systems. Stars cannot be captured.

Chaos certifies inter-system bridges over every independent orbit phase using interval bounds. For irregular formations, measured displacement envelopes around reference polygons are subtracted from available attack range before certification. Swept systems stay at least 420 units apart; foreign systems cannot attack inner capitals. Internal spacing stays at least 250 units under rigid rotation. Quality sweeps cover each system's internal degrees, matching speeds for similar sizes, classic recipe availability, independent orbital phases, distributed 32-bit seeds, real fleet captures, replay reset and camera framing. Chapter 1 definitions and timing remain unchanged; Quick Match geometry is unchanged.

A deterministic seed reproduces the complete map definition, including faction assignment, garrisons, weapon types and orbit phases. Fresh launches use new entropy and avoid repeating the previous seed or layout family. Optional versioned browser history shares that rule across page reloads; corrupt or unavailable storage falls back to the current page session. Replay keeps the generated definition itself and loads a fresh engine, so combat state, fleets, ownership, charges and orbit phase reset together.

## Difficulty and result screens

Hard Mode is the original skull-icon On/Off control restored from commit `13aa334`, with the current audio buttons' hover, focus, sound and transition behavior. It is available for Chapter 1, level tests, Quick Match, and Chaos Mode. Difficulty is fixed when a battle starts and retained across retries, new maps and campaign progression. Hard doubles enemy planet ship production and uses a warmer background; AI decisions, maps and starting ships do not change with difficulty. It defaults to Off on page load.

Only Quick Match and Chaos Mode use the generated-map result actions. Defeat shows New Map, Play Again and Main menu. Victory shows New Map and Main menu. New Map creates a new battlefield in the same mode and difficulty. Play Again resets the exact same opening. Campaign result actions and saved progression remain separate; generated victories never mark a campaign mission complete. Existing chapter-level test options remain in the mode picker.

Each map defines a stable ID, title, briefing, world size, attack range, objective, and planets. Planet coordinates are world units; ships are non-negative integers. Supported owners are the four existing faction colors and neutral gray. Planets can optionally list the superweapons they produce while owned. The current objective is enemy-capital elimination; the player's capital must survive. Validation rejects duplicate IDs, invalid dimensions or owners, invalid weapon lists, missing capitals, and unreachable planets.

To add a level, create another `MapDefinition` and place it at the intended position in the chapter's `maps` array. Do not rename released map IDs. Winning records the ID; selecting Next Mission starts the next array entry. If no next map is released, the player can replay or return to the menu. Chapter 2 contains the five Fractured Galaxy maps described below.

The versioned browser save stores completed map IDs; its legacy selected-mode field is ignored when loading, so every page opening selects Chapter 1. Players can still choose another mode for the current session. Start Chapter 1 always begins with First Strike's tutorial, regardless of previous wins, then victories advance through The Breach Line (Level 2), The Turning Tide (Level 3), The Pincer (Level 4), and The Battle for Helios (Level 5). Retrying a defeat stays on the current level. Starting another run from the menu or refreshing returns to the tutorial, without deleting completed-level records. Existing completion IDs remain valid when a new map is inserted. Invalid saves reset safely; unavailable browser storage shows a warning.

Five text-only Chapter 1 Test options in the mode picker start directly at Levels 1–5, regardless of saved wins. The Level 3 option retains the previous testing route. Victories advance through the remaining Chapter 1 maps in order, using the same maps and rules as the normal campaign; Level 5 ends the run. Test wins do not change saved campaign completion. Selecting normal Chapter 1 still starts at the tutorial.

Checks: `npm run lint`, `npx tsx --test src/game/*.test.ts`, `npm run build`.

## Friendly logistics network

The 600-world-unit range remains a strict source-to-target limit for attacks against enemy and neutral planets. Friendly transfers use the same radius only to determine network connectivity: if two owned planets are joined by any chain of friendly links where every link is 600 units or shorter, ships may fly directly between them regardless of their direct distance. Disconnected friendly planets cannot transfer to each other.

Connectivity is recalculated when the order is issued, using current ownership and live positions. Capturing a bridge can join or split networks, and moving planets can create temporary connections. Once launched, a transfer continues to its destination even if the connection later breaks. Mouse and touch orders share the same rule. Selection and range visuals remain the existing game visuals; this feature adds no network lines or destination rings. Hostile AI logic, Omni-Strike range, and ordinary attack range are unchanged.

Current content: Chapter 1 / First Strike (9 fixed planets), The Breach Line (23 fixed planets and seven Overdrive sites), The Turning Tide (24 planets, one orbiting system and a decorative star), The Pincer (58 fixed planets, a Repulse Shield hub, and six isolated outer branches), and The Battle for Helios (108 planets across five normal stars with 60 orbiting worlds and 48 fixed connecting worlds). Blue and red appear throughout the chapter, green appears in Level 3, and yellow appears in Level 4. Chapter 2 adds Conversion Probe and introduces pink after its first mission.

## Select Mode availability

Select Mode is enabled only on Map 5, Quick Match, and Chaos Mode. Maps 1–4 hide the control and its requirement from both bottom bars, and right-click selection is disabled there. On eligible maps, the main bottom bar shows progress toward the minimum of 30 currently owned planets; the button remains disabled below that threshold. Enemy-planet contextual bars never show Select Mode. Normal and test-level entry points use the same rules.

Button and right-click selection share the ownership requirement. After a successful selection, the mode switches off while selected planets remain highlighted for a friendly-only transfer. Falling below 30 owned planets cancels the group selection and locks the feature again; already launched fleets continue moving. Group transfers cannot attack enemy or neutral planets. Production Overdrive retains its existing map sources and availability.

## Galaxy and camera

Authored maps choose a `galaxyTheme`: First Strike has one faint blue cloud, The Breach Line uses muted violet and rose, The Turning Tide uses teal and violet, The Pincer uses indigo and warm amber, and The Battle for Helios adds a deeper red and amber palette around the central star. Cloud and star counts grow with battlefield area. Quick Match keeps the original palette; Hard Mode uses a slightly redder backdrop and more red clouds. These are visual settings and do not affect combat.

The camera's minimum zoom follows the viewport size relative to the map, so an overview remains available without pulling far beyond a small battlefield. Maximum zoom grows from 1.8 on the tutorial map to 3 on the 3000-unit standalone battlefield. Wheel, pinch, double tap, intro, camera shortcuts, and the end-of-battle camera use those limits.

## First Strike tutorial

First Strike alone opts into an action-driven tutorial via `tutorial.attackTargetId`. It is an authored 1500 × 1500 static map with three blue worlds, four red worlds, and two neutral worlds. Red controls most of the far side, but its outpost directly ahead of the player's capital has only 10 ships and remains the first attack target. The red capital is beyond direct attack range from that outpost, so the player must take another world to reach it. The red central crossing has 14 ships and is the shortest route toward the 70-ship red capital; blue western and eastern harbours offer optional expansion. All nine positions, owners, and starting ship counts are identical on each launch. No orbit, central star, or superweapon is present.

Prompts teach selecting blue then attacking red, scrolling down/pinching fingers together to zoom out, and capturing the red capital while protecting the player's own. They also explain that later battles can have several enemy capitals. Tutorial accents use the existing Chapter 1 cyan-blue (`#73dcff`). Click targets have a circle pulsing every 700ms with no arrow. The zoom prompt shows two matching circles at 58% across/down the viewport; they spread apart and snap back side-by-side every 1.6 seconds while also pulsing in scale, brightness, and glow every 700ms. This requested outward visual cue does not change actual pinch controls. Reduced-motion preferences disable the CSS animations. The win lesson highlights the surviving red capital, not ordinary planets.

Selection immediately advances to the attack prompt. After the first fleet launches, the tutorial hides for four active-play seconds before showing the zoom lesson, leaving time to watch the attack. Pausing also pauses this delay; later attacks cannot restart it. A manual zoom completed during the delay is remembered, but the capital explanation still waits until the delay ends. Retry creates a fresh tutorial state and there are no delayed browser timers to leak into the next mission.

The zoom lesson and two-circle cue are touch-only (`hover: none` and `pointer: coarse`). PC players go directly from the post-attack delay to the capital explanation, with a two-step counter instead of three; mouse-wheel zoom still works normally. Detection is based on input capability rather than viewport width, so shrinking a desktop window does not enable the touch lesson.

The simulation runs continuously during every tutorial prompt, including the initial selection and attack instructions: ships move, planets produce units, and AI keeps playing. Only the player's normal Pause control stops gameplay. The camera settles on the player's opening fleet on both desktop and mobile. Selecting/deselecting updates the first prompt; a real launch advances it, and manually zooming out to the map overview advances to the win explanation on touch devices. The intro camera never completes that lesson. Players can skip at any time or dismiss the final explanation with Got it. The tutorial restarts on mission retry/replay and does not change campaign saves. Other missions and Quick Match have no tutorial. The old persistent mission title/objective overlay and pause-menu mission briefing are removed from every level.

## Mission 2: The Breach Line

A 2500 × 2500 static battlefield rotated into a west-to-east confrontation. Blue starts with only its capital and 220 ships. Red starts with four eastern worlds: its 125-ship capital, the 48-ship gate, and the upper and lower 35-ship outposts. The other 18 planets are neutral, including all seven central Overdrive worlds. Planet positions, starting ship counts, and attack range are unchanged.

- Central route: capture the cheap neutral supply world to reach the western Overdrive site. The exact center planet is also a marked Overdrive world. Another marked site and a red gate lead toward the eastern capital.
- Upper route: neutral harbour, entry, and relay worlds lead into the central ring or through the neutral bastion and approach toward the red outpost.
- Lower route: a matching neutral flank leads into the ring or toward the red lookout.
- All seven marked planets sit within 500 units of the map center and produce only Overdrive. Neither faction starts with a weapon source. One captured site generates a charge in 60 seconds, two take 30 seconds, and seven take about 9 seconds. Losing all sites pauses future generation; an already earned charge remains available. For 7.5 seconds, Overdrive sets one owned planet's total production to the combined normal production of all other currently owned producing planets, with its own normal rate as a minimum. Dyson spheres and active Overdrive bonuses are excluded from that total. The rate follows territory changes each production cycle, retains Hard Mode production bonuses, and respects the faction ship cap and low-garrison allowance.

Winning First Strike starts The Breach Line in the current run. Winning The Breach Line starts The Turning Tide. No tutorial prompts repeat after Mission 1.

## Mission 3: The Turning Tide

A 2600 × 2600 battlefield, with 12 fixed outer worlds and 12 rotating worlds around one white star. Rotation remains clockwise, once every 180 seconds. Both enemy capitals must fall; the blue capital must survive.

- Opening: blue, red, and green each hold only their capital. Blue has 260 ships and three cheap nearby choices: two outer harbours and the boarding planet. Red and green each have 200 ships and can take a cheap outpost, an orbiting Overdrive world, or the contested northern divide.
- Moving route: board the outer orbit, develop the four inner worlds, and use passing planets as staging positions. All 12 orbiting worlds share angular speed; connections to the fixed outer ring open and close. Both rotating rings alternate Omni Strike and Production Overdrive worlds.
- Fixed routes: the west and east flanks provide permanent, more heavily defended alternatives to riding the orbit.
- Pressure: red and green expand from their single northern capitals into neutral territory. Their orbiting footholds now start neutral, so control of the ring develops during the battle. The northern divide is affordable and reachable from both enemy capitals, inviting an early contest between them.
- Reward: capturing marked rotating worlds unlocks their weapon and generates charges while held. The center is a normal star and cannot be captured.

The full orbit stays clear of fixed planets. Every screen briefly shows the complete map, then approaches the southern capital. PC framing includes the nearby flanks and routes into the ring; phones focus on immediate expansion choices. Normal panning and zoom remain available.

## Mission 4: The Pincer

A 6000 × 6000 static battlefield with 58 planets: a seven-world central hub, three ten-world enemy branches, and three seven-world weapon branches. Three enemy branches alternate with three weapon branches around the center. Blue starts with only its 360-ship capital; red, green, and yellow each start with a 220-ship capital and three controlled rear worlds holding 12, 16, and 12 ships. The 45 existing neutral worlds stay neutral. The released map ID, campaign ordering, and Level 4 test option are preserved.

- Center: the blue capital and six neutral hub planets all produce Repulse Shields. Each neutral hub has 12 defenders, offering cheap opening choices. Blue initially has one shield source; taking all six neutral hubs raises that to seven sources and a charge approximately every nine seconds. Enemies start without weapon sources. Repulse Shield protects an owned world from incoming fleets for six seconds.
- Three fronts: red approaches from the north, green from the southeast, and yellow from the southwest. Each enemy area has a 3×3 block plus an inward entrance. The original gate and capital rows remain in place relative to the hub; the new controlled row sits 560 units behind the capital row. Each capital still has three affordable neutral neighbors: its inward gate and two side wings. The larger border translates all existing positions equally, preserving their spacing and frontline connections. Enemies must expand through their branch before contesting the central shields. Capturing all three rival capitals while preserving blue wins the mission.
- Weapon objectives: the northeast group contains seven Production Overdrive sites, the southern group seven Omni Strike sites, and the northwest group seven Repulse Shield sites. All begin neutral, with 18–32 defenders. Each group has an entrance, a gate, a rear vault, and two pairs of flank worlds. Taking a whole group supplies seven sources for its weapon, encouraging investment and later contests over those branches.
- Frontlines: every branch joins exactly one hub shield through its entrance, then passes through a gate before reaching the wider outer territory. The outer rows have two flank routes, but neither route bypasses the entrance or gate. Removing the central hub leaves six disconnected groups: three with ten enemy-area worlds and three with seven weapon worlds. There are no direct range links between outer groups.
- Spacing: attack range remains 600. Intended links are 520–560 world units long, while every unintended planet pair is at least 700 units apart. This prevents attacks across branches, past a gate, or diagonally behind a frontline. Normal attacks and Omni Strike obey the same geometry; friendly transfers continue to use the existing connected ownership network. Automated tests check every pair against the complete intended connection graph, test entrance/gate cuts, and exercise actual fleet and weapon range checks.

Every screen briefly shows the full map using the authored `overviewScale: 0.68`, then approaches the blue capital. PC framing includes the shield hub and the six branch entrances; phones keep the six opening shields readable. `capitalFocusY: 0.5` centers the hub vertically above the command bar. The existing map-scaled zoom limits support both views. The indigo and amber background retains the Pincer palette, with more clouds and stars for its larger area. Winning The Turning Tide starts The Pincer, and winning The Pincer starts The Battle for Helios.

The released map ID remains unchanged so saves, chapter progression, and the Level 5 test option still work.

- Opening: blue, red, green, and yellow each own only one capital, orbiting their own corner star. Blue starts with 300 ships; each rival starts with 240. All 104 other worlds start neutral. Each capital faces inward, with two neighboring 12-ship weapon sites and affordable inner/outer expansion choices. No faction starts with weapon production; capturing nearby sites opens those tools.
- Connectivity: 48 fixed planets connect the systems. Four perimeter lanes permit attacks between neighboring factions without capturing Helios. Diagonal approaches branch into those lanes and the central routes. Four crossings join the outer lanes to a continuous 16-world necklace around the center. The fixed network stays connected if any single fixed world is removed. Each system has several entrances that stay within attack range of some outer-ring planet at every rotation phase.
- Central objective: all 12 central orbiting worlds produce weapons, alternating Omni Strike, Production Overdrive, and Repulse Shield. Eight nearby fixed necklace worlds also produce those weapons. These 20 weapon sites are neutral at the start, with 28–44 defenders; the remaining necklace links have 22. Owning multiple sites speeds up weapon charging under the existing weapon rules. ## Camera and HUD transitions

Opening and Space-to-capital camera moves share a world-space focus and proportional zoom transition. Overviews account for the measured top and bottom controls and preserve authored overview scales. All maps approach the capital on every screen, including Quick Match and Chaos Mode. Phone framing fits planets within one attack range; PC framing (viewport width at least 700 px) also fits planets within two attack ranges to reveal nearby routes. The destination follows live planet geometry and control sizes rather than a fixed zoom multiplier. The legacy `mobileFocus` map field no longer suppresses PC intros. The capital shortcut uses exactly the same destination framing.

Intros show the overview for 220 ms, then approach the capital over 450–1600 ms according to travel distance and zoom ratio. Capital recalls and bookmarks have no overview hold; reduced motion and stationary transitions skip it. Orbiting capitals are resolved after each simulation step. Resizing preserves the current world center and retargets active transitions for the new viewport and control sizes without restarting a completed overview hold. Pause freezes camera transitions, and hidden-tab time does not consume them. Reduced motion skips the approach and HUD slides. Manual mouse wheel, pinch, drag, and bookmark controls remain available; manual camera input cancels an active recall.

The top and bottom HUD panels enter together with transform/opacity transitions. Hidden controls are inert. Friendly/enemy command content updates in one stable container without queued exit/entry animations. Offscreen ships and planets skip drawing with padding for glows, effects, and weapon badges; rotation-aware viewport bounds retain edge visuals and leave simulation state unchanged.

## Orbiting systems

An optional `orbit` defines a single fixed star center (`x`, `y`), clockwise period in simulation seconds (`periodSeconds`), and participating `planetIds`. Maps with several stars use `orbits` instead. The Turning Tide retains its single 180-second system; The Battle for Helios uses five independent systems. Members of each system share angular speed, preserving spacing and mutual attack ranges. Decorative stars have no effect on gravity, collision, damage, or victory. An optional `dysonSphere` still supports one legacy capturable sphere per map, though no current Chapter 1 map uses it.

Stationed ships and their local movement targets rotate with their own system. Launched ships pursue moving targets in world space; captures do not stop rotation. Pausing stops rotation, retry resets positions, and end-of-battle slow motion applies to all systems. Quick Match generates fixed home areas around one or two moving orbital circuits; Chaos Mode generates fully orbiting star systems. Validation rejects mixed single/multiple definitions, empty systems, duplicate or shared membership, invalid periods, star intersections, and swept map-edge intersections. An authored orbit may opt into `allowDelayedBoarding` to start undocked; validation still requires its swept radius to reach a fixed planet connected to the playable map. Regression tests cover independent rotation rates, fleets, central weapon charges, opening captures, clearance, connectivity, and all three rival capitals.

### Conversion Probe

- One stored charge; a source takes 60 seconds. Multiple sources increase charging up to four effective sources (15-second minimum). The button shows actual source count and recharge time.
- Launch at any hostile or neutral non-capital, non-Dyson world within 600 units of any owned planet. A faction must still have its capital. Duplicate probes from the same faction to one target are prevented.
- A distinct probe leaves the capital at 225 world units per second, twice the average normal attacking ship speed. It follows the target's live position, including orbiting worlds. Its hull, glow, exhaust, and fading trail use the sender's faction color. There is no destination line, target ring, or arrival countdown.
- Successful arrival triggers a 1.2-second pink halo, expanding capture wave, twin turning arcs, and short radial streaks in the shared planet-effects renderer. It follows the captured planet, freezes with the simulation, and clears on expiry, recapture, or retry. Reduced motion keeps a steady, fading halo and arcs without rotation, expansion, or radial streaks.
- Impact captures the planet and changes all live stationed ships to the attacker's color. Launched fleets retain their original allegiance. Target weapons remain available to the new owner.
- If the firing faction captures the destination before impact, the probe explodes at its current flight position in its faction color. It cannot recapture the friendly planet or trigger the pink arrival halo; the spent charge remains spent.
- Repulse Shield cannot block conversion. It continues to repel normal fleets and Omni Strike ships. Evacuating a threatened garrison denies those ships to the conversion attacker.
- Launch spends the charge. Losing launch range after launch does not cancel it; losing the launching capital does. An already-friendly target cancels harmlessly. Capitals remain immune.
- AI factions can launch probes at shielded worlds and reserve their shields for incoming combat fleets.

### Pink construction

Pink (`#ff8fc7`) appears from Mission 2 and uses the same fleet combat, production, and charge rules as other factions. Its additional strength is transforming non-capital, non-Dyson planets it continuously holds. A visible ring and countdown show the work: at 30 seconds, existing weapon sources are stripped; at 60 seconds, the planet produces only Conversion Probe. Plain planets follow the same construction timeline. An existing exclusive conversion source needs no work.

Capturing a planet resets unfinished construction. Weapons already stripped remain removed. Completed conversion sources persist after capture and benefit the new owner. Capitals never transform. This behavior is enabled only on the three Chapter 2 maps that include pink. Charge integration splits at construction boundaries so newly built sources cannot receive charge retroactively.
